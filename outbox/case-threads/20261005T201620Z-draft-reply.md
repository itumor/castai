## Q1 — Why the well-configured cluster shows a minus

On **dema-platform-services-test** the Workload Autoscaler line for August shows **−$24.81 (≈ −€22)** — exactly the "couple of dozen Euro in minus" you are seeing. Meanwhile the cluster's total realized savings for August were **+$213**. On the console these lines appear side by side, which makes the small negative sub-line look like the cluster's result.

**Why this line is negative by construction.** Its formula is:

```
Workload Autoscaler savings = (original requests − current requests) × max(current price, baseline price)
```

On your clusters the Workload Autoscaler mostly **raised** requests that were set too low (an availability/stability correction for under-provisioned workloads). When current requests are *higher* than the original ones, the delta is negative by design — it is the honest accounting of requests you were previously under-requesting, not wasted money. Two things to keep in mind when reading it:

- This figure is **already included in Total savings** — it is a sub-attribution, not an additional charge on top.
- It typically turns positive once rightsizing catches up. On `dema-platform-services-test` it flipped positive on **September 24**, and October so far is **+$5.87**.

For completeness, a ranked checklist of realistic causes for a negative savings figure on a well-configured cluster — with how each one maps to your two clusters:

1. **Workload Autoscaler raising previously under-set requests** — *verified as the actual cause on both of your clusters* (details above).
2. **Baseline window effects** — savings only accrue after the baseline period ends; a view crossing that boundary can show flat or negative stretches. Your baselines closed on July 22, so current windows are unaffected.
3. **CAST AI fee included in net numbers** — if you are looking at a net-of-fee view, the platform fee is deducted from gross savings; a small gross figure can turn net-negative. Worth checking which view your tile shows.
4. **List-price vs. discounted (EDP) unit rates** — baseline unit costs are always computed with discounted pricing; mixing a list-price view of actual cost with the discounted baseline can distort the difference. *(All figures in this reply use discounted pricing.)*
5. **Commitment/RI/Savings Plans coverage** — heavily covered capacity has a low effective rate; if the model prices replacement capacity at a higher effective rate in a window, a sub-line can dip negative even though nothing got worse.
6. **Spot interruption or replacement churn** — replacement/fallback capacity priced higher within a short window can push a few days negative. Not applicable today: both clusters currently run 100% on-demand.
7. **Rightsizing recommendations not yet applied (workloads not in VPA "apply" mode)** — recommendations alone do not change requests, so there is no positive rightsizing effect to offset sub-line corrections.
8. **Storage / egress / static overhead** — the savings model accounts for CPU and RAM demand only; storage, network, and other fixed costs sit in actual spend without a savings counterpart, which can skew narrow views.

## Q2 — Karpenter on nodes, CAST AI on workloads: do savings appear, and which ones?

Yes, they appear — and precisely these:

- **Reported as: Workload Autoscaler savings.** CAST AI rightsizes your workload requests (CPU/RAM); Karpenter then provisions fewer or smaller nodes to fit the reduced demand. The savings are computed from the request reduction itself: `(original requests − current requests) × max(current effective price, baseline price)`. This value is calculated independently of the node management layer — it even accrues during a cluster's baseline period — and is shown once the Workload Autoscaler manages at least 20% of workloads in VPA mode.
- **Not reported as: node autoscaling savings.** Realized (node-level) savings measure CAST-managed node capacity — downscaling, bin-packing, spot adoption — and activate only when CAST AI manages at least 20% of the nodes. With Karpenter owning all node provisioning, that share is 0%, so no node-level savings are attributed to CAST AI. That is correct behavior: the node-level efficiency on those clusters is Karpenter's work, which you will see reflected in your AWS bill, not in CAST AI reporting.

In short for your setup: **Karpenter's effect stays in your cloud bill; CAST AI's effect (smaller requests → Karpenter provisions less capacity) shows up in CAST AI reporting under Workload Autoscaler savings.** The two are complementary, and there is no double-counting between them.

Public documentation for both formulas and the adoption thresholds:
- Realized savings report: https://docs.cast.ai/docs/savings-report
- Savings calculations (baseline & formulas): https://docs.cast.ai/docs/savings-baseline
